How to Price Mobile Mechanic Jobs (Labor Rate, Parts Markup & Trip Fee)

Most new mobile mechanics underprice. They charge what feels fair, then find out the drive, the parts run and the comeback ate the profit. Here is a simple way to price a job so it actually pays.

1. Start from an hourly rate that covers your costs

Add up a normal month of costs: tools and tool payments, insurance, phone, fuel and vehicle wear, supplies, software and the tax you set aside. Divide by the hours you can really bill in a month, not the hours you are busy. Driving, quoting and parts runs are usually not billable. Then add what you want to earn per hour on top. Our free mechanic labor rate calculator does this math for you.

2. Quote labor from book time, not the clock

Shops usually price labor from a labor guide's published time for the job, so the customer pays the same whether it takes you longer or you are quick. If you don't use a labor guide yet, keep a log of how long each job type really takes you and quote from that.

3. Decide how you handle parts

  • You supply parts: many shops add a markup to cover sourcing time, returns and warranty hassle. Write the part price and any markup on the estimate.
  • Customer supplies parts: say up front that you don't warranty customer-supplied parts, and consider a slightly higher labor rate.

4. Add a trip or service-call fee

Coming to the customer has a cost. A flat trip fee, or a fee by distance zone, covers your drive and stops "just come look at it" visits from being free. Many mobile mechanics credit the fee toward the repair if the customer approves the work.

5. Put it in a written estimate before you start

  • Labor lines with hours and rate
  • Parts lines with prices
  • Trip fee, shop supplies and tax
  • An approval line, plus "call me before anything over $___"

A signed or texted approval protects you if the job grows. Check your state's auto repair rules, since many states have estimate and authorization requirements. See what to put on an auto repair invoice for the full checklist.

Worked example

Front brake pads and rotors, 1.5 book hours at $85/hr$127.50
Pads and rotors (your cost $110 + 20% markup)$132.00
Trip fee$35.00
Shop supplies (brake clean, grease)$8.00
Estimate before tax$302.50

These numbers are only an example. Your rate, markup and trip fee depend on your costs and your local market.
