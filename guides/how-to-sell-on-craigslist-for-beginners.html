How to Sell on Craigslist for Beginners

Craigslist is a free local classifieds site where you post an ad in your city and buyers contact you by email or text. It works well for bulky items that are a pain to ship, like furniture, tools, appliances and auto parts, and for anything you want gone the same day. This guide walks through your first sales without guessing at rules that change by city.

Craigslist rules, categories and posting limits vary by area. Check your city's Craigslist help pages before you post.

1. Pick the right city and category

Open the Craigslist site for the metro where buyers can actually pick up, not a city hours away. Choose the category that matches the item, such as furniture, tools, auto parts, electronics or general for sale. A clear category puts you in front of people who are already looking for that kind of thing.

2. Know what sells on Craigslist

Heavy, bulky and local-use items move well: couches, desks, exercise gear, lawn equipment, tools, car parts and working electronics. Brand-name clothing and small collectibles often do better on eBay, Poshmark or Depop. Search recent ads in your city for the same item so you know the going local price. See how to source items at thrift stores and how to inspect items before you list.

3. Write a clear title and description

Lead with brand, item and the one detail buyers search for, such as size, model or year. In the body, state condition, exact measurements if they matter, what is included, and any flaws. Say whether the price is firm or if you will take offers, and how you prefer to meet. Keep personal info out of the ad; use Craigslist's anonymous email relay when you can. See how to write listing titles and how to write item descriptions.

4. Use honest photos

Take several well-lit photos that show the whole item, close-ups of labels or model numbers, and every scratch or stain. Craigslist buyers decide from pictures first, and surprises at pickup kill the deal. A plain wall and daylight beat a cluttered garage shot. See how to take listing photos.

5. Price from local comps, with room to haggle

Search Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace and OfferUp in your area for the same item, and check eBay sold listings for a national floor. Local buyers expect to negotiate, so list a little above the lowest price you will take after your cost, gas and time. There is usually no platform fee on a cash local sale, but your miles and time still count. See how to price from sold comps and how to sell locally on Facebook Marketplace.

6. Reply fast and screen buyers

Answer serious questions quickly with short, clear replies. Be wary of anyone who wants to pay with a strange payment app, asks you to ship far away with a prepaid label they send, or pushes you off the platform into a sketchy story. Prefer local pickup, cash or another payment method you already trust, and confirm the meetup details in writing.

7. Meet safely and finish the sale

Meet in a public, well-lit place when the item is small enough to bring, or have another person with you for home pickups. Show the item, let the buyer inspect it, and do not release it until payment is in your hand or clearly settled. Once it sells, delete or mark the ad so you do not get more messages, and end the same listing on OfferUp or Marketplace if you cross-listed it. See how to sell on OfferUp for beginners and how to cross-list without double-selling.

8. Log every sale

Record what you paid, the sale price, gas or parking for the meetup, and any packing or cleaning supplies. Local sales look "fee-free," but miles and no-shows still eat profit. Tracking each flip shows which categories are worth your time. See how to calculate real profit after fees.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

You buy a working microwave at a garage sale for $15. Similar ones on Craigslist nearby are listed at $40 to $55. You list at $45, accept $40 cash at a grocery-store parking lot, and drive four miles round trip. After gas, you log the real profit in your tracker, not just the $40 cash.

Common mistakes

  • Vague titles like "misc stuff" that nobody searches for.
  • Hiding flaws in photos, which leads to angry buyers at pickup.
  • Pricing with no room to haggle, then walking away from fair offers.
  • Meeting alone in a private place for a high-value item.
  • Forgetting to delete the ad after it sells, which wastes time on more messages.
