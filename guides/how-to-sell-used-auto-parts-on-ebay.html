How to Sell Used Auto Parts on eBay

Pulled parts, leftover parts from a job, and parts from a parts car can all turn into cash on eBay. Car parts buyers are picky, though. They want to know the part will fit their vehicle, and they will return it if it does not. Getting the part number, fitment and shipping right is most of the work.

1. Find the part number first

Look for the manufacturer part number stamped, cast or printed on the part or its label, and write it down before you clean or pack anything. Buyers and eBay search both lean on part numbers, and the number is the fastest way to check fit and look up what the part sells for. Photograph the number clearly so the buyer can check it too.

2. Fill in fitment

eBay lets you add a compatibility list showing which years, makes, models and engines a part fits. Buyers search by their own vehicle, so a listing with fitment shows up in more searches and gets fewer "will this fit?" questions. Only list vehicles you can confirm from the part number or a parts catalog, since wrong fitment leads straight to returns.

3. Write a title buyers search for

Put the year range, make, model, part name, side or position, and part number in the title, for example "OEM", the part name, and "driver side" or "front" where it applies. See how to write eBay listing titles.

4. Describe the condition honestly

Say where the part came from, whether it was working when removed, and any damage, wear, missing tabs or broken connectors. Mention the mileage of the donor vehicle if you know it. Show every flaw in the photos. See how to take eBay listing photos and how to write eBay item descriptions.

5. Price from sold listings

Search the part number and filter to sold items to see what buyers actually paid. Compare like for like: same condition, same included hardware. Then check that the price still leaves a profit after fees and shipping. See how to calculate real eBay profit after fees.

6. Weigh and pack before you set shipping

Auto parts are heavy, oily and oddly shaped, so guessing shipping is how most part flips lose money. Drain fluids, bag anything greasy, protect connectors and sensors, and box the part before you weigh and measure it. Large items can get expensive fast because of size, not just weight. See how to pack items for shipping and how to ship items cheaply on eBay.

7. Check the rules for restricted parts

Some items, like batteries, airbags, parts that still hold fluids or fuel, and anything with refrigerant, have extra rules from eBay and from shipping carriers. Read eBay's current policy and the carrier's rules before listing anything in those groups.

8. Set a clear return policy

Car parts get returned more than most items, usually for fit. A clear fitment list and part number photo prevent most of them. Decide your policy up front and know how you will handle a return. See how to handle returns on eBay.

9. Track profit per part

If the parts came off one parts car, split what you paid for the car across the parts you sell, or track the car as one cost and count every sale against it until it pays off. Either way, log every sale with fees and shipping so you know whether the parts car actually made money.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

You pull a headlight assembly off a parts car. The part number on the back matches a model range, so you list it with that fitment and the number in the title. Sold listings show what the same headlight goes for in similar shape. You box it with plenty of padding, weigh it, and set shipping from the real numbers. When it sells, you log the price, fees and shipping against the parts car so you can see how much of the car is paid off.

Common mistakes

  • No part number in the photos or title.
  • Guessing fitment instead of confirming it.
  • Guessing shipping on a heavy or bulky part.
  • Shipping parts that still hold fluids or that need special handling.
  • Not tracking the parts car as a cost, so you never know if it paid off.
