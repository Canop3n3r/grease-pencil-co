How to Reinvest Reseller Profits

A sale feels like spending money, but most of it is not profit yet. Part of it is the cost of the item you already paid, part is fees and shipping, part is taxes you will owe later, and only what is left is real profit. Splitting every sale the same way keeps your inventory growing without running out of cash or getting surprised at tax time.

1. Know your real profit on each sale

Start from what actually landed in your account after platform fees and the shipping label. Subtract what you paid for the item and any supplies. What is left is the profit you get to split. See how to calculate real profit after fees.

2. Put the item's cost back first

Before anything else, move the amount you paid for the item back into your buying money. That keeps your inventory budget from shrinking every time something sells. If you skip this step, a few good weeks can still leave you with less to buy with than you started with.

3. Set aside taxes and supplies

Reselling profit is usually taxable income, so put a share of each sale's profit aside right away instead of waiting until the end of the year. Keep a small amount for tape, mailers, labels and gas too. See how much to set aside for side-hustle taxes.

4. Pick a simple split for the rest

Decide ahead of time what share of the remaining profit goes back into inventory and what share you keep. Early on, many resellers put most of it back in to grow; later, they pay themselves more. The exact numbers matter less than using the same rule every week so you are not deciding on impulse.

5. Reinvest in what is already selling

Look at what sold fastest and with the best return, and buy more of that kind of item. Avoid spending new money on categories that are still sitting unsold. See how to calculate sell-through rate and how to calculate ROI.

6. Watch money tied up in old stock

Add up what you paid for everything still unsold. If that number keeps climbing while sales stay flat, slow down buying and work on stale listings first. Cash sitting on a shelf cannot buy the next good find.

7. Do it once a week

Pick one day, total the week's sales, and move the money into its buckets: item costs back to buying money, taxes, supplies, reinvestment and your pay. Ten minutes a week is enough. See how to track side-hustle income and expenses.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

This week you sold five items and $180 landed in your account after fees and labels. You had paid $45 for those items and used $10 in supplies, so profit is $125. You move $45 back to buying money, set aside $30 for taxes and $10 for next week's supplies, and split the other $85 using your rule of 70% back into inventory and 30% for you: about $60 more to buy with and about $25 in your pocket. Next week you have $105 to source with instead of $45.

Common mistakes

  • Spending the whole sale and forgetting the item's cost was your money to begin with.
  • No tax set-aside, then a big bill in spring.
  • Reinvesting in slow sellers because a deal looked good, not because that category sells.
  • Changing the split every week based on mood instead of a rule.
  • Not tracking cost per item, so you cannot tell profit from money you are just getting back.
