How to Write eBay Item Descriptions That Sell

Your title and photos get a buyer to click. The description is what gets them to buy without a question, and to keep the item once it arrives. You don't need sales copy. You need the facts a buyer would ask about, in the order they'd ask. This guide gives you a short template that works on eBay, Poshmark and Mercari.

Each platform has its own rules about what can go in a listing and how item specifics work; check its current seller help pages.

1. Lead with what it is

Start with one plain sentence that repeats the key facts from your title: brand, item, model, size or color, and the overall condition. Many buyers read on a phone and only see the first line or two before scrolling, so make those lines count. Your title does the searching; see how to write eBay listing titles that get found.

2. Describe the condition honestly

  • Name every flaw: stains, scratches, pilling, missing buttons, odors, fading. Say where it is and point to the photo that shows it.
  • Use specific words. "Small scuff on the right side, about the size of a pencil eraser" beats "minor wear."
  • Don't oversell. "Like new" sets an expectation the item has to meet when it arrives.

Honest flaws cost you fewer sales than you'd think, and they save you returns. See how to handle returns on eBay and how to take eBay listing photos that sell.

3. Give measurements and specs

  • Clothing: pit to pit, length, sleeve, waist, inseam, rise. Tag sizes vary by brand and age.
  • Shoes: tag size plus insole length if you can measure it.
  • Electronics: model number, storage or capacity, and what version or region it is.
  • Parts and tools: part number, fitment info you can verify, and dimensions.

Fill in item specifics too. They help your listing show up in filtered searches and answer questions before they're asked.

4. Say exactly what's included

List what's in the box: cables, charger, remote, manual, original box, accessories. If something isn't included, say so in plain words, especially if a buyer might expect it. "Console only, no controller" prevents a "not as described" claim.

5. Say how you tested it

For anything that plugs in, turns on or moves, say what you actually checked. "Powers on, reads discs, both controllers sync" is far more convincing than "works great." If you couldn't test it, say that clearly and price it accordingly.

6. Add shipping and handling details

One line on how fast you ship and how you pack it builds trust, especially for fragile items. Keep it accurate to what you'll really do. See how to ship items cheaply on eBay.

A simple copy-and-paste template

[Brand] [item] [model], [size/color]. [Overall condition].

Condition: [each flaw, where it is, which photo]
Measurements / specs: [numbers]
Tested: [what you checked and how]
Included: [everything in the box; what's not included]
Shipping: [handling time and packing]

Example (made-up item for illustration only):

Logitech G Pro wired gaming mouse, black, tested and working. Pre-owned in good condition.

Condition: Light shine on the left button and a small scuff on the right side (shown in photo 5). Cable has no cuts or kinks. Feet are intact.
Tested: Plugged into a Windows PC; all buttons, scroll wheel and lights work.
Included: Mouse only. No box, software disc or extra weights.
Shipping: Ships within 1 business day, packed in bubble wrap in a box.

Message me with any questions before buying.

Common description mistakes

  • Leaving it blank or writing only "see photos."
  • Vague condition words like "good used condition" with no details.
  • Copying a stock description that doesn't match your item's condition or contents.
  • Keyword stuffing or listing other brands the item isn't.
  • Long walls of text buyers won't read on a phone.
  • Promising what you didn't test.
  • Forgetting to update the description when you relist after a return.
