How Much to Set Aside for Side-Hustle Taxes (Simple Monthly Method)

Side-hustle money usually comes with no tax withheld, so April can sting. A simple monthly habit fixes that.

The 5-minute monthly method

  1. Add up what came in this month: sales, cash jobs and app transfers.
  2. Subtract business expenses: parts, inventory, supplies, fees and tools.
  3. Subtract your mileage deduction: business miles × the standard mileage rate for the year (published by the IRS each year).
  4. That’s your profit. Multiply it by your set-aside percentage.
  5. Move that amount into a separate savings account right away.

What percentage?

Many side hustlers use somewhere around 25–30% of profit as a starting rule of thumb, because self-employment tax applies on top of income tax. Your real number depends on your income, state and situation, so a tax professional can help you pick it. Setting aside a little too much is a nicer surprise than too little.

Quarterly estimated payments

If you expect to owe a meaningful amount, the IRS generally expects estimated payments during the year, typically around mid-April, mid-June, mid-September and mid-January. Check IRS Form 1040-ES for the exact dates and rules for your situation.

Keep the records

  • A mileage log made at the time of the drive: date, purpose, and start and end odometer readings
  • Receipts for expenses
  • A record of every payment you received

This is general information to help you stay organized. It is not tax advice.
