How to Inspect and Test Items Before You Resell Them

The cheapest mistake in reselling is the one you catch before you pay. A missing piece, a hidden stain or a gadget that will not power on can turn a good flip into a return or a dead item on the shelf. A two-minute check at the store, and a slightly longer one at home before you list, saves most of that.

1. Check before you pay, not after

Look the item over in good light before it goes in your cart. Turn it over, open every door and zipper, and look inside. If you would have to explain a flaw to a buyer, factor it into your price now, using the max buy price rule. If the flaw makes it hard to sell at all, put it back.

2. Clothing and shoes

Check armpits, collars, cuffs and seams for stains, holes, pilling and loose stitching. Smell it, since smoke and musty smells are hard to remove. Test every zipper and button. Read the tag for size and fabric, and note if a tag is missing or cut. On shoes, look at the soles, heel wear and the inside lining.

3. Electronics and small appliances

Many thrift stores have an outlet or testing station. Use it if they allow it, and plug in only items that look undamaged, with no frayed cords, burn marks or swelling. Make sure it powers on and the main features work. Check for the charger, remote, cables and battery cover, since missing accessories lower the price a lot. If you cannot test it, price it as untested or skip it.

4. Tools

Look for cracks, rust, worn bits and missing guards. For cordless tools, find out whether the battery and charger are included, since a bare tool sells for less. Check that triggers, chucks and adjustments move freely. Test corded tools only where it is safe to do so.

5. Toys, games and sets

Sets, puzzles, board games and building kits sell best when complete. Count pieces when you can, check for the manual and box, and look for battery corrosion in the battery compartment. Sell an incomplete set as a lot or for parts and say so clearly.

6. Housewares and decor

Run a finger around rims and edges to find chips you cannot see. Hold glass up to the light for cracks. Look on the bottom for a maker's mark, since it helps with pricing and with writing the title.

7. Be careful with brand-name items

Counterfeits show up in secondhand stores too, especially with designer bags, sneakers and electronics. Compare logos, stitching, labels and serial numbers to photos of the real item from the brand's own site. If you are not sure it is real, do not sell it as that brand. Marketplaces can remove listings and suspend sellers over counterfeit items.

8. Check for recalls and banned items

Some items, especially baby products, car seats and certain toys, can be recalled or restricted. Check the product on the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission recall list at cpsc.gov before buying to resell, and read the platform's rules for restricted items.

9. Clean, recheck and note every flaw

Clean the item at home, then look again, because cleaning can reveal scratches or stains you missed. Write each flaw down, photograph it, and put it in the description. Buyers who know about a flaw up front rarely return the item for it. See how to take eBay listing photos and how to write eBay item descriptions.

10. Log what you found

Write the condition notes next to the item in your inventory record. When something does come back, you will know what you saw before it shipped. See how to handle returns on eBay.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

You find a stand mixer at a thrift store. You plug it in at the testing outlet and it runs at every speed, but the whisk attachment is missing. You check sold listings on your phone and see that mixers without all attachments sell for noticeably less. You lower your max buy price to match, buy it, and list it with the missing whisk named in the title and description. It sells with no return because the buyer knew exactly what they were getting.

Common mistakes

  • Checking at home instead of in the store, after the money is spent.
  • Assuming it works because it looks clean.
  • Missing accessories like chargers, remotes and batteries.
  • Selling a brand-name item you have not checked for authenticity.
  • Leaving flaws out of the listing, which invites returns.
