How to Batch List Items for Resale (A Weekly Routine for eBay, Poshmark & Mercari)

Listing one item at a time is slow because you keep switching jobs: clean, set up the lights, shoot, look up comps, type, measure, repeat. Batch listing means doing each job for a whole pile of items before moving on. You set up once, get into a rhythm, and items that would have sat in a "to list" pile for weeks go live in one session.

1. Pick a set listing time each week

Block the same two or three hours every week for listing, and treat it like a shift. Items only earn money once they are listed, so a regular session beats waiting until you "feel like it". Start with a realistic goal, such as 10 or 15 items, and raise it as you get faster.

2. Prep the whole pile first

Pull everything from your "to list" spot. In one pass, clean, steam or wipe each item, check it for flaws, test anything that powers on, and set aside anything that is not worth listing. Note flaws on a sticky note on the item so you remember them when you write the description. If you have not set up a storage spot yet, see how to organize reseller inventory at home.

3. Photograph everything in one setup

Set up your background and light once, then shoot item after item without moving anything. Take the same set of angles every time (front, back, label or model number, close-ups of flaws, measurements) so you never have to reshoot. See how to take listing photos that sell.

4. Measure and weigh as you go

While each item is in front of you, write down its measurements and packed weight. Having the weight ready means you can set shipping correctly without digging the item back out. See how to ship items cheaply.

5. Research comps in a block

Look up sold prices for the whole pile in one sitting instead of between photos. Write a list price and a floor (the lowest you will take after fees) for each item. See how to price items to sell on eBay and how to handle offers.

6. Write listings from a template

Keep a saved title pattern and a description template, then fill in the blanks for each item. Most of the words stay the same; you only change the brand, size, condition notes and measurements. See how to write listing titles and how to write item descriptions.

7. Tag, log and shelve before you stop

As each listing goes live, give the item its SKU, add a row to your inventory sheet with what you paid, your list price, your floor and the date listed, and put it in its numbered bin. Finishing this in the same session keeps your sheet and shelves matching. See how to track reseller inventory in a spreadsheet. If you sell on more than one site, see how to cross-list.

Example (made-up details for illustration only):

On Sunday afternoon you pull 12 items from the "to list" bin. You spend 30 minutes cleaning and checking them, 40 minutes photographing all 12 against the same wall, 30 minutes looking up sold prices, and about an hour writing listings from your template. Before you stop, each item has a SKU, a row in your sheet and a spot in a bin. Twelve items went live in one afternoon instead of trickling out over a month.

Common batch-listing mistakes

  • Mixing jobs, like stopping to research one item in the middle of a photo session.
  • Skipping measurements and weights, so you have to pull items back out later.
  • Pricing without a floor, then guessing when offers come in.
  • Leaving logging for "later", so the sheet stops matching the shelves.
  • Setting a goal so big that you dread the session and skip it.
