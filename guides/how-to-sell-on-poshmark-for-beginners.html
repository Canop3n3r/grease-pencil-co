How to Sell on Poshmark for Beginners

Poshmark is a social marketplace best known for clothing, shoes, bags and accessories, though it also handles home goods and some electronics. You list from your phone, buyers get a prepaid shipping label, and part of selling is sharing your listings so they stay near the top of search. It pairs well with eBay and Mercari if you already resell.

1. Set up your closet

Your shop on Poshmark is called a closet. Add a clear profile photo, a short bio that says what you sell, and a bank account for payouts. Turn on two-step sign-in. A closet that looks cared for gets more trust than an empty profile.

2. Check sold prices first

Search for your item and filter to sold listings to see what it actually went for. Asking prices on Poshmark are often high because sellers expect offers, so sold prices are the real guide. See how to price items to sell; the sold-comps method works here too.

3. Photograph clothes so buyers can judge fit

Use natural light and a plain background or a hanger on a clean wall. Show the front, back, brand tag, size tag, fabric tag and any flaws up close. Your first photo is the cover shot buyers see in feeds, so make it the cleanest one. See how to take listing photos.

4. Write a listing buyers can search

Put the brand, item type, style name, size and color in the title. In the description, give measurements (pit to pit, length, inseam), the fabric, the condition and every flaw. Honest measurements are the best way to avoid returns over fit. See how to write listing titles and how to write item descriptions.

5. Price with room for offers

Most Poshmark buyers make offers, and you can send offers to people who like your items. Set your list price a little above the price you actually want, and decide your lowest acceptable price before you list. See how to handle offers for a simple counter-offer approach.

6. Know the fees and shipping before you set a price

Poshmark takes a commission from each sale, and buyers usually pay shipping with a prepaid label that has a weight limit. Fees and label rules change, so check Poshmark's current pages before pricing, and compare platforms in eBay vs Poshmark vs Mercari fees. Heavy items can go over the label weight limit, so weigh them packed first.

7. Share your listings

Sharing pushes a listing back toward the top of feeds and search. A few minutes of sharing your closet each day, especially newer listings, helps buyers see your items. Share other sellers' items too; it is part of how the community works.

8. Offer bundle discounts

Buyers can bundle several items from your closet into one order with one shipping label. Setting a bundle discount encourages bigger orders and saves you trips to the post office. See how to sell items in bundles and lots.

9. Ship fast and pack neatly

Print the prepaid label and ship within a day or two. Fold clothes neatly in a clean mailer; tidy packing earns good ratings and repeat buyers. See how to pack items for shipping.

10. Track your real profit

Write down what you paid, the sale price, the Poshmark fee and any supplies for every item. If you cross-list, remove the item everywhere the moment it sells. See real profit after fees and how to cross-list on eBay, Poshmark and Mercari.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

You buy a name-brand jacket at a thrift store for $10. Sold listings show similar jackets going for about $45 to $55. You list at $58, a buyer offers $45, you counter at $50 and they accept. The buyer pays for the prepaid label. After you subtract Poshmark's fee, a $1 mailer and your $10, you write the actual profit in your tracker, not the $50 sale price.

Common mistakes

  • Pricing from asking prices instead of sold ones.
  • Leaving out measurements, which leads to fit returns.
  • Listing at your bottom price with no room for offers.
  • Never sharing, so listings sink out of sight.
  • Counting the sale price as profit instead of what is left after fees and costs.
