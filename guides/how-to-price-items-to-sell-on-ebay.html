How to Price Items to Sell on eBay (Using Sold Comps)

The asking price on active listings tells you what sellers hope to get. The price on sold listings tells you what buyers actually paid. Price from sold comps and most of the guesswork goes away.

Step 1: Pull sold comps, not active listings

On eBay, search for your item, then turn on the Sold items filter (in the filter list on the left, or under Advanced search). Sold prices show in green. If you have a seller account, the Product research tab in Seller Hub shows the same kind of sold data over a longer window. On Poshmark and Mercari, filter the search to sold items too.

Look at the last few months, and aim for at least five sales of the same item. If you can only find one or two, treat any price as a guess and list a little higher to leave room.

Step 2: Match your item, not just the name

Throw out comps that are not really the same thing. Keep the ones that match on:

  • Exact model, size or version, since a different year or size can be a different price.
  • Condition: new with tags, used, or for parts are three different markets.
  • What is included: box, manuals, cables, accessories.
  • Shipping: compare total cost to the buyer. A $25 item with free shipping and a $17 item plus $8 shipping cost the buyer the same.

Step 3: Pick a price from the middle

Line up the matching sold prices and ignore the oddballs at each end. The middle of what is left is your realistic sale price. If you want a faster sale, list near the low side of the middle. If you are happy to wait, list near the high side and accept offers.

Worked example

These numbers are made up to show the method. Say you found a used video game controller and pulled these sold comps (total paid by the buyer, same model, used, no box):

Sold comp 1$18.00
Sold comp 2$24.00
Sold comp 3$25.00
Sold comp 4$26.00
Sold comp 5$27.00
Sold comp 6$29.00
Sold comp 7$45.00 (new in box, so thrown out)

Drop the new-in-box sale, since it is a different item. The $18 sale looks like a quick dump, so set it aside too. The middle of the rest is about $26. A sensible plan: list at $29.99 with offers on, and treat $25 as your floor.

Step 4: Set your floor before you list

Your floor is the lowest offer you will accept. Work it out from what you keep, not from the sale price: subtract platform fees, shipping and supplies, then check the profit still beats what you paid by enough to be worth the time. The free reseller profit calculator does this in one step, and the eBay fee calculator and Poshmark and Mercari fee calculator show the fee on any price. Decide the floor once and you will not second-guess every lowball offer.

Step 5: Adjust if it sits

If an item has had plenty of views but no sale after a few weeks, the price is usually the problem. Check fresh sold comps, then drop the price in small steps or send offers to watchers. If it gets almost no views, the title, photos or category are more likely the issue than the price (see how to write eBay listing titles that get found). Tracking how long each item has been listed makes this easy to spot; see how to track reseller inventory in a spreadsheet.

Price before you buy, too

The same sold-comp check is how you decide what to pay in the first place. Run it in the store, then use the max buy price calculator and the sell-through rate calculator to see whether the item is worth buying and how fast it moves.
