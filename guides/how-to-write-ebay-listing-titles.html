How to Write eBay Listing Titles That Get Found

Buyers find most listings by typing words into a search box. If your title does not contain the words they type, a good item at a good price can sit for weeks with almost no views. The title is the cheapest fix in reselling: it costs nothing and takes two minutes.

The formula

eBay gives you up to 80 characters for a title. Fill them in roughly this order, most important words first:

  1. Brand (Nike, Pyrex, DeWalt, Levi's)
  2. What the item is, in the plain word a buyer would use (jacket, mixing bowl, drill, jeans)
  3. Model, line or style name or number (Air Max 90, 401, DCD771, 501)
  4. Key details: color, material, pattern, year or era, features
  5. Size or capacity when it matters (Men's 10.5, 2.5 qt, 32x30)
  6. Condition or extras if there is room (NWT, new in box, with charger, lot of 3)

Good and bad examples

These are made-up listings to show the idea.

The stronger titles say exactly what the item is, so they match more searches and the right buyer clicks.

Where to find the right words

  • Sold comps. When you pull sold listings to set your price, look at the titles of the ones that sold. Borrow their wording, not their exact title. Our guide on how to price items using sold comps walks through finding them.
  • The search bar suggestions. Start typing the item into the search box and note the phrases it suggests. Those are common searches.
  • The label, tag or bottom of the item. Model numbers, style names and pattern names are often printed right on it, and collectors search by them.

Mistakes that bury a listing

  • Filler words like L@@K, WOW, RARE or MUST SEE use up space and nobody searches for them.
  • Leaving out the brand or model when you know it.
  • Using your own word instead of the buyer's word, such as "sweater top" when buyers search "cardigan".
  • Brand names that are not yours to use. Do not add another brand to borrow its traffic (for example "like Lululemon"). Marketplaces treat that as keyword spam and can remove the listing.
  • Wasting the space. If you have used 40 characters, there is usually a size, color or feature you can still add.

Fill in item specifics too

eBay also asks for item specifics such as brand, size, color and model. Fill in every one that applies. Buyers use the filters on the left of the search page, and a listing with blank specifics can drop out when they filter. Poshmark and Mercari have similar fields for brand, size and category.

Title, photos, then price

If an item gets almost no views, fix the title and category first, then the photos (see how to take eBay listing photos that sell). If it gets views but no sale, the price is more likely the problem.
