Car Maintenance Schedule by Mileage (Simple Checklist + Free Printable Ideas)

Most car trouble that gets expensive starts as maintenance that got skipped. Here is a simple way to think about what's due and when. Your owner's manual is the real schedule for your car. Use the ranges below as a starting checklist and check them against your manual.

The rule: whichever comes first

Most items have two limits: miles and time. An oil change might be "every 5,000 miles or 6 months, whichever comes first." If you don't drive much, time is usually what triggers it.

Common starting intervals

ItemTypical range to check against your manual
Engine oil & filterOften 5,000–10,000 miles with synthetic, or about every 6–12 months. Older cars and severe driving (towing, short trips, heat, dust) need it sooner.
Tire rotationOften every 5,000–7,500 miles, a good time to check pressure and tread too
Engine air filterOften 15,000–30,000 miles, sooner on dusty roads
Cabin air filterOften 15,000–25,000 miles or yearly
Brake inspectionAt least yearly, or any time you hear squealing or grinding
Brake fluidMany manufacturers say every 2–3 years
CoolantVaries widely, from about 30,000 miles on older cars to 100,000+ on long-life coolant
Transmission fluidVaries widely. Some manuals say 30,000–60,000 miles, others call it "lifetime." Check yours.
Spark plugsOften 30,000 miles for copper plugs and 60,000–100,000 for iridium or platinum
Serpentine beltInspect regularly; many are replaced somewhere between 60,000 and 100,000 miles
Wiper blades, battery testAbout yearly; most batteries last roughly 3–5 years

How to keep track without thinking about it

  1. Write down every service with the date and odometer reading, even DIY oil changes.
  2. For each item, next due = last done + interval (in miles and in months).
  3. Check the list once a month, or whenever you fill up.

Why a service record pays off

When you sell, a log with dates and mileage shows a buyer the car was looked after, and it gives you something concrete to point to when you set your price. It also helps with warranty claims on parts, since you know when and where you bought them.

This is general information, not mechanical advice. Follow your owner's manual and a trusted mechanic for your vehicle.
