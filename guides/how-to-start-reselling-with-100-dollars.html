How to Start Reselling With $100 (A Simple First-Month Plan)

You do not need a garage full of stock to start flipping. With about $100 you can buy a small batch of items, learn what sells in your area, and turn profit back into more inventory. The goal of the first month is not to get rich; it is to learn your numbers and build a habit without losing the starting money.

1. Split the $100 before you spend any of it

Set aside a small part for supplies (tape, poly mailers, a few boxes, a cheap scale if you do not have one) and keep the rest for inventory. Hold back a little cash for shipping labels if your platform charges them up front. Write the split down so you are not tempted to spend it all at the first thrift store.

2. Start with small, light items you understand

Cheap, light, easy-to-ship items let you make more flips with the same money and keep shipping simple. Pick a category you already know something about, such as clothing brands you wear, video games, tools or kitchen gear. You will spot good buys faster and describe them better.

3. Check sold prices on your phone before you buy

Look up what the item has actually sold for, not what people are asking. Check how many have sold recently compared with how many are listed, so you know it moves. See how to price items to sell and how to calculate sell-through rate.

4. Set a max buy price and stick to it

Take the likely sale price, subtract platform fees, shipping, supplies and your gas, and decide the least profit that is worth your time. Whatever is left is the most you should pay. Walk away from anything above it. See how much to pay for items to resell and the free max buy price calculator.

5. Buy a small batch, not one big item

Spreading the money over several cheap items is safer than one expensive gamble. If one does not sell, the others still bring cash back in. Good places to look include thrift stores, garage sales and clearance racks. See how to source items at thrift stores and how to find items at garage sales and estate sales.

6. List everything within a few days

Items only earn money once they are listed. Clean, photograph and list the whole batch in one session. See how to batch list items, listing photos that sell and listing titles that get found.

7. Write down every cost and every sale

Log what you paid, where you bought it, your list price, what it sold for, fees and shipping. Without this you cannot tell if you are actually making money or just moving it around. See how to calculate real profit after fees and how to track inventory in a spreadsheet.

8. Put profit back in and repeat

When items sell, return the original cost to your inventory money first, then decide how much profit to reinvest. Do more of what sold fast and less of what sat. Remember to keep some aside for taxes once you are earning; see how much to set aside for side-hustle taxes.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

You keep $15 for mailers, tape and labels and take $85 shopping. You buy eight items averaging about $10 each, checking sold prices for every one and skipping anything above your max buy price. You list all eight that weekend. Over the month six sell; after fees and shipping they bring back more than the $85 you spent. You return the $85 to your inventory money, reinvest part of the profit, and note which two items sat so you avoid them next time.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Buying from asking prices instead of sold prices.
  • Spending everything on one item that might not sell.
  • Forgetting fees, shipping and gas, so a "profit" turns out to be a loss.
  • Letting items sit unlisted in a pile for weeks.
  • Not tracking anything, so you cannot tell what is working.
