How to Keep Car Maintenance Records (So You Get More When You Sell)

A car with a clean service history is easier to keep running and easier to sell. Buyers trust it, mechanics can see what was done, and you stop guessing when the next oil change is due. Here is a simple system that takes about a minute after each service.

What to write down after every service

  • Date and odometer reading
  • What was done, in plain words (oil and filter, front pads, tire rotation)
  • Parts and fluids used, with brand and part number when you have it
  • Who did it: you, a shop, or a mobile mechanic
  • Cost, split into parts and labor if you can
  • When it is due next, by miles and by months, whichever comes first

Keep receipts in one place

  1. Pick one spot: a folder in the glovebox, an envelope at home, or a phone folder of photos.
  2. Snap a photo of every receipt the day you get it. Paper fades and gets lost.
  3. Name photos by date and mileage, like 2026-10-22_84120_oil.jpg, so they sort themselves.
  4. Write the receipt's total into your log so the log is the one place you check.

Track fuel and costs too

Fill-ups tell you a lot. If your miles per gallon suddenly drops, something like low tire pressure, a dragging brake or a dirty air filter may be the cause. Log the odometer and gallons at each fill-up and work out MPG with our free MPG and gas cost calculator. Add up fuel, repairs and insurance to see what the car really costs you per mile with the free cost per mile calculator.

Know what is due next

Check your owner's manual for the real intervals, then put the next due mileage for each item at the top of your log. Our car maintenance schedule by mileage covers the common items, and the free oil change due calculator tells you the date or mileage for your next one.

Why it pays when you sell

When you list the car, a one-page service summary plus the receipts answers the buyer's biggest question: was this car taken care of? It gives you a reason to hold firm on price, and it helps a private sale move faster. If the car needs a big repair first, run the numbers with the free repair or replace calculator before you decide.
