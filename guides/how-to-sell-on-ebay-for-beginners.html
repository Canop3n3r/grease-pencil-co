How to Sell on eBay for Beginners

eBay is still the biggest general marketplace for used goods. Almost anything sells there, from clothes and electronics to auto parts and collectibles, and buyers search it the way they search Google. This guide walks through your first sales step by step, and links to deeper guides for each part.

1. Set up your account the right way

Create a seller account, add a bank account for payouts, and turn on two-step sign-in. Fill in your profile and return address. New sellers often start with selling limits that rise as you build a track record, so start with a few items you know well rather than a big batch.

2. Start with what you already own

Your first listings should be things around the house you no longer use. It costs you nothing, teaches you the listing and shipping process, and builds feedback before you spend money on inventory. See how to start reselling with $100 for the next step.

3. Look up sold prices before you list

Search for your item and filter to sold items. That shows what buyers actually paid, which matters far more than what other sellers are asking. See how to price items to sell on eBay.

4. Take clear photos

Use natural light and a plain background. Show every side, labels and model numbers, and any flaws up close. The first photo is the one buyers see in search, so make it the cleanest. See how to take eBay listing photos.

5. Write a title buyers will search

Lead with brand, model, item type, size or key spec, and color. Skip filler words like "wow" or "look". Fill in the item specifics too, because buyers filter by them. See how to write eBay listing titles and how to write eBay item descriptions.

6. Choose Buy It Now or auction

Buy It Now with offers turned on works for most everyday items: you set the price and can accept, decline or counter offers. Auctions can work for rare or in-demand items where buyers compete. When in doubt, start with Buy It Now priced from sold comps. See how to handle offers on eBay.

7. Know the fees and shipping before you price

eBay takes a final value fee from each sale, and fees and promotions change, so check eBay's current fee page before you price. Weigh and measure the item packed before you set shipping, so the cost does not eat your profit. See how to ship items cheaply on eBay and eBay vs Poshmark vs Mercari fees.

8. Ship fast and pack well

Ship within your stated handling time, upload tracking, and pack so nothing shifts in the box. Fast, careful shipping is the easiest way to earn good feedback early. See how to pack items for shipping.

9. Set a return policy you can live with

Decide whether you accept returns and who pays return shipping. Accurate descriptions and honest flaw photos prevent most returns. See how to handle returns on eBay.

10. Track your real profit

For every item, write down what you paid, the sale price, eBay's fee, shipping and supplies. The sale price is not your profit; what is left after all of that is. See real eBay profit after fees and how to track reseller inventory in a spreadsheet.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

You list an old video game controller from your closet. Sold listings show it going for about $30 to $35. You list it Buy It Now at $34 with offers on, and accept a $30 offer. You charge shipping that covers the label. After you subtract eBay's fee and a $1 padded mailer, you write the actual profit in your tracker, not the $30 sale price.

Common mistakes

  • Pricing from asking prices instead of sold ones.
  • Guessing shipping instead of weighing the packed item.
  • Hiding flaws, which leads to returns and bad feedback.
  • Listing too much at once before you know the process.
  • Counting the sale price as profit instead of what is left after fees and costs.
