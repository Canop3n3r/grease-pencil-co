How to Take eBay Listing Photos That Sell

Your title gets a buyer to the listing. Your photos decide whether they stay. You do not need a camera, a lightbox or editing software; a phone, a window and a plain background are enough for most thrift and garage sale finds.

The setup (about five minutes)

  1. Light. Shoot near a window during the day, with the window to the side of the item, not behind it. Turn off overhead yellow bulbs so the colors stay true. Skip the flash; it causes glare and hard shadows.
  2. Background. Use something plain and uncluttered: a white poster board, a sheet, a clean floor or a blank wall. Plain backgrounds make the item the only thing to look at.
  3. Clean the item and the lens. Wipe off dust and lint, and wipe your phone lens. A smudged lens is the most common cause of hazy photos.
  4. Hold steady. Brace your elbows on a table or use a cheap phone stand if you already have one. Tap the screen on the item so the phone focuses on it.

The shot list

eBay lets you add up to 24 photos on a listing, and buyers expect to see the whole item. A good default order:

  1. Main photo: the whole item, straight on, filling most of the frame. This is the thumbnail in search results.
  2. Back and sides, so nothing is hidden.
  3. Tags and labels: brand tag, size tag, model number plate, pattern name or the stamp on the bottom. These back up what your title says.
  4. Close-ups of details buyers care about: zippers, buttons, ports, soles, the inside of a bowl.
  5. Every flaw, close up, with something for scale if it helps (a finger or a coin).
  6. Proof it works for electronics and tools, such as the item powered on, when that is true.
  7. Everything included in one shot: the item with its charger, case, manual or lot pieces laid out together.

Show flaws honestly

A clear photo of a small stain or scratch, plus a line in the description, is the best protection against "not as described" returns. Buyers are usually fine with flaws they were shown; they are not fine with surprises. Do not edit flaws out or use filters that change the color, and do not use stock photos from the manufacturer for used items.

Mistakes that make buyers scroll past

  • Dark or yellow photos from overhead lights at night.
  • Clutter in the background: laundry, pets, other items for sale.
  • A tiny item in a big frame. Move closer rather than zooming, which blurs the image.
  • Only one or two photos on an item buyers would want to inspect.
  • A blurry main photo. It is the one that shows in search, so retake it until it is sharp.

Photos, title, then price

If an item gets views but no watchers or offers, the photos may not be answering the buyer's questions; add the missing angles and tag shots. If it gets almost no views, start with the title and category (see how to write eBay listing titles that get found). If it gets views and watchers but no sale, look at price with how to price items using sold comps, and check what you would still keep with the free reseller profit calculator.
