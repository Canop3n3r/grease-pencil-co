How Much to Mark Up Auto Parts (Markup vs Margin for Mechanics)

Parts markup pays for the time you spend sourcing parts, the returns run when the counter gives you the wrong one, and the comeback when a part fails under warranty. Here is how to pick a markup, why markup and margin are not the same number, and a worked example.

Markup vs margin in one minute

  • Markup is profit as a percent of your cost. A $40 part sold for $60 is a 50% markup ($20 / $40).
  • Margin is profit as a percent of the selling price. The same $40 part sold for $60 is a 33.3% margin ($20 / $60).
  • The two formulas: margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup), and markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin).

The trap: if you want to keep 40% of every parts dollar and you add 40% to cost, you only get a 28.6% margin. To actually keep 40% you need a 66.7% markup.

A simple tiered markup (example)

A flat percentage overcharges on big parts and undercharges on small ones. Many shops use a sliding scale instead: a higher percent on cheap parts, a lower percent on expensive ones. The tiers below are example numbers to show the idea, not a recommendation for your market.

Your costExample markup
Under $25100%
$25 to $10050%
$100 to $30035%
Over $30025%

Worked example

Using the example tiers above:

  • An oil filter that costs you $12 is billed at $24 (100% markup, 50% margin, $12 profit).
  • A brake rotor that costs you $48 is billed at $72 (50% markup, 33.3% margin, $24 profit).
  • An alternator that costs you $180 is billed at $243 (35% markup, 25.9% margin, $63 profit).

Check your billed prices against what a customer would pay at a local parts store. If you are far above retail, customers notice, so pull that tier back down.

When the customer brings their own parts

You lose the parts profit and you can't warranty a part you didn't source. Common ways to handle it: charge a higher labor rate for customer-supplied parts, state in writing that the part carries no warranty from you, or decline jobs where a bad part would mean a free second visit. Whatever you choose, put it on the estimate before you start.

Show parts clearly on the estimate

List each part as its own line with quantity and your billed price, not your cost. Customers approve faster when they see what they're paying for. The full walkthrough is in how to write a car repair estimate, and setting your labor side is covered in how to price mobile mechanic jobs.
