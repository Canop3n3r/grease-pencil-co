How to Price Items to Sell on Mercari

Price too high and your Mercari listing sits. Price too low and the fee and shipping eat your profit. This guide shows a simple way to price an item on Mercari from real sold comps, with your costs built in, so you know your floor before any buyer sends an offer.

Mercari's selling fee, shipping options, offer tools and policies change over time. Check Mercari Help for the current numbers before you rely on any figure here.

1. Start with sold comps, not asking prices

Search for your exact item on Mercari and filter to Sold. Active listings show what sellers hope for; sold listings show what buyers paid. Match brand, size, model and condition, and look at the last several sales. If few items have sold, check eBay sold listings for a second opinion. See how to price from sold comps.

2. Add the fee and shipping to your math

Mercari takes a selling fee out of the sale, and shipping is either paid by the buyer or built into your price. Look up the current fee in Mercari Help, then work backward: sale price, minus fee, minus shipping you cover, minus supplies, minus what you paid. What is left is real profit. See eBay vs Poshmark vs Mercari fees and how to calculate real profit after fees.

3. Set your floor price first

Your floor is the lowest price that still leaves the profit you want after fee, shipping and supplies. Write it down before you list. Having a number in advance keeps you from accepting a lowball offer just to get a notification to stop buzzing. See how much to pay for items to resell.

4. List a little above your floor to leave room for offers

Mercari buyers like to make offers and use price-drop alerts. List above your floor by a margin you are comfortable giving up, so a fair offer still lands at or above your floor. If you refuse to negotiate at all, say so in the description, and expect fewer messages.

5. Decide who pays for shipping

Free shipping to the buyer often looks more attractive, but it means you pay for the label. Heavy items can quietly wipe out profit this way. Weigh and measure the packed item, check the current shipping rate in the app, and compare the two options before you decide. See how to pack items for shipping.

6. Handle offers calmly

Counter once or twice rather than accepting the first number. Anything at or above your floor is a win; anything below, politely decline. Bundle discounts can help when a buyer likes several of your items, but check the bundle still clears your floor on each. See how to sell items in bundles and lots.

7. Drop the price on a schedule, not on a whim

If an item has had no sales after a few weeks, lower the price in small steps, refresh the photos or title, and recheck sold comps. Stop dropping once you hit your floor. If it will not sell there, it may do better on another site, or it may be time to donate it and move on. See how to cross-list.

8. Log every sale to learn what to buy next

Record what you paid, the sale price, the fee, shipping, supplies and days to sell. After a few weeks, patterns show up: which categories clear your floor fast, and which just tie up cash. The Reseller Profit Tracker is built for exactly this.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

You buy a pair of boots for $12. Recent sold comps on Mercari are $35 to $45. Suppose the fee and shipping you cover total about $9 and supplies are $1. To keep $15 profit, your floor is $12 + $9 + $1 + $15 = $37. You list at $44, counter a $34 offer at $40, and the buyer accepts. You still clear your floor, and you log the real profit in your tracker.

Common mistakes

  • Pricing from active listings instead of sold ones.
  • Forgetting the fee and shipping when you calculate profit.
  • Having no floor price and accepting any offer.
  • Offering free shipping on heavy items without checking the label cost.
  • Dropping the price too fast or below your floor.
  • Never rechecking comps when an item stalls.
