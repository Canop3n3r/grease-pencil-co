How to Write a Car Repair Estimate (Mobile Mechanic Example)

A clear written estimate is how a mobile mechanic gets the yes, gets paid without arguments, and looks like a real shop. Here is what to put on one, how to price it, and a full worked example you can copy.

What every repair estimate needs

  • Your business details: name, phone, and email so the customer can call back with a yes.
  • Customer and vehicle: name, phone, year, make, model, VIN or plate, and current mileage.
  • The complaint and what you found: the customer's words ("grinding when braking"), then your finding ("front pads at 2mm, rotors scored").
  • Each job as its own line: labor hours, labor rate, and the parts for that job, so the customer can approve some jobs and decline others.
  • Fees and tax: shop supplies, trip fee, disposal, and sales tax where your state charges it.
  • An expiration date: parts prices move, so say how long the quote is good for, for example 14 days.
  • Approval and signature: a line saying no work beyond this estimate without the customer's OK, with a signature and date.

How to price each line

  1. Labor: use book time for the job times your hourly rate. Not sure what to charge per hour? The free mechanic labor rate calculator works it out from your real costs.
  2. Parts: your cost plus a markup that covers sourcing runs, returns and warranty. Many independents mark parts up somewhere around 20% to 50%, so pick a rule and use it every time.
  3. Fees: a trip fee for driving to the customer and a small shop supplies charge for cleaners and consumables.
  4. Tax: rules on taxing parts and labor differ by state, so check yours. The example below taxes parts only at an example rate.

Worked example: front brake pads and rotors

A 2015 sedan with grinding front brakes. All rates here are example numbers, not a recommendation.

Labor: 1.5 hr book time at $80/hr$120.00
Parts: pads $45 + 2 rotors at $55 = $155, with 30% markup$201.50
Shop supplies (brake cleaner, grease)$10.00
Trip fee$25.00
Subtotal$356.50
Sales tax on parts (8.25%, example rate)$16.62
Estimate total$373.12

Show the customer this breakdown, not just the bottom line. Line-by-line pricing answers "why so much?" before they ask it.

Getting the yes (and protecting yourself)

  1. Send it before you start. A text or email of the estimate with a "reply YES to approve" works, but a signed form is stronger.
  2. Split must-do from can-wait. Mark each line as safety, soon, or optional. Customers approve more when they can choose.
  3. Call before going over. If you find a seized caliper mid-job, stop, add it as a new line, and get approval before doing it.
  4. Turn it into the invoice. When the job is done, the approved estimate lines become your invoice. See what to put on an auto repair invoice.
