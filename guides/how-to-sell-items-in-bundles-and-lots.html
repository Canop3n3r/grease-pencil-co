How to Sell Items in Bundles and Lots

Not every item is worth listing on its own. Some things sell for so little that the time to photograph, list and ship them eats the profit. Grouping those items into a bundle or lot can turn a pile of slow, cheap stock into one sale worth your time. Bundling the right way also gets buyers to spend more per order.

1. Know when to bundle

Bundle items that are low value on their own, slow to sell, or that buyers naturally want together. Kids' clothes in one size, a set of matching dishes, books by one author, or a box of small tools often sell better as a group. Keep strong single items separate, since a good item can hide inside a lot and sell for less than it would alone.

2. Group items a buyer would want together

A good lot has a clear theme: same size, same brand, same series, same hobby or same use. "Boys size 6 winter clothes, 8 pieces" is easier to search for and easier to say yes to than a random mix. Put the theme and the count in the title. See how to write eBay listing titles.

3. Price the bundle from your numbers

Add up what you paid for every item in the lot, then work out the fees and shipping for the whole package. Price the bundle so the profit after fees and shipping is worth one listing and one packing job. Check sold listings for similar lots, not single items, to see what buyers actually pay. See how to price items to sell on eBay.

4. Offer bundle discounts to buyers

eBay, Poshmark and Mercari each have their own ways to let buyers combine items from your store, and some let you set a discount for buying more than one item. Turning that on gives buyers a reason to add another item to the order, and you only pack and ship once. Check each platform's current settings, since the options change.

5. Photograph the whole lot clearly

Lead with one photo that shows every item in the lot laid out together, so the buyer can count them. Follow with close-ups of the best pieces and of any flaws. List every item in the description so there are no surprises. See how to take eBay listing photos.

6. Weigh the packed lot before you list

Lots get heavy fast, and shipping can wipe out the profit if you guess. Pack the lot, weigh and measure it, and use the real numbers for the shipping price. See how to pack items for shipping.

7. Use bundles to clear stale stock

If single items have sat for months, pulling them together into a themed lot is often faster than marking each one down again. It frees shelf space and turns stuck cash back into sourcing money. See how to handle stale eBay listings.

8. Track bundle profit as one sale

Log the total cost of every item in the lot against the single sale price, fees and shipping. If you track items individually, mark each one as sold in the bundle so your inventory count stays right. See how to track reseller inventory in a spreadsheet.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

You have six toddler shirts in the same size that you paid $1 each for. Listed one at a time, each might sell for a few dollars, and you would pack and ship six times. Instead you list them as one "size 2T shirts, 6 pieces" lot. After fees and shipping you clear more on that one sale than the six small ones would likely have made combined, with one listing and one trip to drop it off.

Common mistakes

  • Hiding a valuable item inside a cheap lot.
  • Random mixes with no theme that are hard to search for.
  • Guessing the weight of a heavy lot and losing money on shipping.
  • Not listing every item in the description, which leads to returns.
  • Forgetting the cost of every item when working out lot profit.
