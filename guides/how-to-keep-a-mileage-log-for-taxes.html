How to Keep a Mileage Log for Taxes (Gig Drivers, Resellers & Mobile Mechanics)

If you drive for a side hustle, your miles can be one of your biggest deductions. They only help if you can back them up. Here is what a good mileage log looks like and a simple way to keep one.

What each entry should have

  • Date of the trip
  • Where you went and the business purpose ("thrift run, Goodwill and two garage sales", "brake job at customer's house", "DoorDash shift")
  • Start and end odometer readings, or the miles for the trip
  • Your total miles for the year, business and personal, so you can show the business share

The IRS expects records made at or near the time of the trip. A log you rebuild from memory in April is much weaker than one you filled in the same day. See IRS Publication 463 for the official rules.

Which trips usually count

  • Resellers: sourcing runs, post office drop-offs, trips to buy supplies
  • Mobile mechanics: driving to customers, parts-store runs, trips to a machine shop
  • Gig drivers: miles while working a delivery or rideshare shift, as each app and your tax pro define it

Your regular commute from home to a main workplace usually does not count. If you have a home office as your main place of business, trips from home to business stops often do. A tax professional can tell you how this applies to you.

Standard mileage rate or actual expenses

Most side hustlers use the standard mileage rate: business miles × the rate the IRS publishes each year. It is simple and only needs the mileage log. The other choice is actual expenses (gas, repairs, insurance, depreciation, times your business-use percentage), which takes far more receipts. Whichever you use, there are rules about switching between them, so check before you pick.

A 30-second habit that keeps it current

  1. Snap a photo of the odometer when you start and finish a business trip.
  2. Once a day or once a week, type the trips into one log.
  3. At month end, total the business miles and multiply by the year's rate to see what they are worth.
  4. Move part of your profit into a tax savings account the same day.
