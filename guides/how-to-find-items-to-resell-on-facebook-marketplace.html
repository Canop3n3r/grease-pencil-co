How to Find Items to Resell on Facebook Marketplace

Local listing apps like Facebook Marketplace are full of people who just want something gone this week. That makes them a good place to buy inventory without leaving home until pickup time. The trick is searching smarter than everyone else, checking real sold prices before you message, and keeping every pickup safe and logged.

1. Search for sellers in a hurry

Besides searching item names, try words that signal a quick sale, such as "moving," "must go," "garage cleanout," "lot," "bundle," "storage unit" or "estate." Sort by newest so you see fresh listings before other flippers do, and widen your search radius only as far as you are willing to drive.

2. Try misspellings and vague titles

Listings with misspelled brand names, blurry photos or titles like "box of stuff" get fewer views, so they often sell cheaper. Search common misspellings of the brands you know, and open the "lot" and "bundle" listings to look at every photo.

3. Save searches and check at set times

Save the searches that match what you sell and turn on notifications, then check at a few set times a day instead of scrolling all day. Good deals go fast, but a short routine beats burning hours on the app.

4. Check sold prices before you message

Look up what the exact item has actually sold for online, not what other people are asking locally. If it rarely sells compared with how many are listed, it may sit. See how to price items to sell and how to calculate sell-through rate.

5. Set your max price, then make a polite offer

Work out the most you can pay and still make the profit you want after fees, shipping and gas. Send a short, friendly message with a real offer and a pickup time you can actually make. Sellers usually pick the buyer who is easy to deal with. See how much to pay for items to resell and the free max buy price calculator.

6. Keep pickups safe

Meet in a busy public place in daylight when you can; many towns have a police station or store parking lot that people use for this. Tell someone where you are going, bring exact cash, and do not go inside a stranger's home alone if you are not comfortable. If a deal or a seller feels off, walk away.

7. Inspect before you pay

Count pieces, look for cracks and stains, check serial numbers when it matters, and test anything that powers on if the seller lets you. Every flaw you find now is one you can price in and mention honestly in your listing. See how to handle returns.

8. Log the buy before you forget

Write down each item, what you paid, the date, where you got it and the miles you drove. Split a bundle price across the items so each one has a cost, then give each item a spot and a code. See how to keep a mileage log, how to organize reseller inventory and how to track inventory in a spreadsheet.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

A listing titled "moving, box of video game stuff" shows a console, three controllers and a stack of games for $60. You check sold prices for the console and the two games you can read in the photos, decide your max is $50, and offer $45 for a pickup that evening at a store parking lot. The seller takes it. You test everything on the spot, log the $45 and 14 miles, split the cost across the pieces, and list them over the weekend.

Common mistakes

  • Messaging before checking sold prices, then overpaying because you already committed.
  • Driving too far for a small profit once gas and time are counted.
  • Skipping the inspection because the pickup felt rushed.
  • No-showing or lowballing after agreeing, which burns sellers you may want to buy from again.
  • Letting the haul sit instead of listing it that week. See how to batch list items.
