How to Source Items to Resell at Thrift Stores (In-Store Checklist)

Good flips are made at the shelf, not at the listing. If you pay too much, buy something that won't sell, or miss a flaw, no title or photo will save the profit later. This checklist keeps each buy decision to a minute or two, so you leave with items that sell instead of a cart full of maybes.

What sells, and for how much, changes by item, season and platform. Always go by recent sold listings, not what other sellers are asking.

1. Check sold comps before you put it in the cart

Search the item on your phone and filter to sold or completed listings. Look for items in the same condition, with the same model or size. Asking prices tell you what sellers hope for; sold prices tell you what buyers actually paid. See how to price items to sell on eBay.

2. Make sure it actually moves

A high sold price doesn't help if one sold last year and fifty are listed right now. Compare how many sold recently with how many are listed. If listings far outnumber sales, expect a long wait. See how to calculate sell-through rate.

3. Inspect it like the buyer will

  • Clothing: stains, holes, pilling, broken zippers, missing buttons, odors, and whether the tag is still there.
  • Electronics: test it if the store has an outlet, and check for missing chargers, cables, remotes or battery covers.
  • Sets and games: count the pieces; missing parts usually sell for much less.
  • Anything else: cracks, chips, rust, and whether it will survive shipping.

Every flaw you find now is one you can either price in or walk away from, instead of a return later. See how to handle returns on eBay.

4. Work out your max buy price

Start from a realistic sold price, then take off platform fees, shipping you'll pay, supplies, and the minimum profit you want for your time. What's left is the most you should pay. If the tag is higher, put it back. The free max buy price calculator does the math, and how much to pay for items to resell explains it step by step.

5. Think about shipping before you buy

Heavy, bulky or fragile items can eat the whole profit in postage and packing. A light item that fits in a padded envelope is often a better flip than a bigger sale price that needs a large box. See how to ship items cheaply on eBay.

6. Count your time and gas

A store 40 minutes away has to produce more profit to be worth it. Keep a rough idea of your profit per sourcing trip, not just per item, and log the miles if you track them for taxes. See how to calculate ROI on reselling and how to keep a mileage log.

7. Write down what you paid right away

Snap the receipt or jot each item's cost before you leave the parking lot. If you wait until you list, you'll forget, and your profit numbers will be guesses. See how to track reseller inventory in a spreadsheet.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

You find a name-brand fleece tagged $9. On your phone, similar ones in the same size and condition sold for around $35, and more have sold than are listed right now. It has no stains and the zipper works. After fees, about $8 of shipping and a padded mailer, and a $12 minimum profit, your max buy price works out to about $10. The $9 tag is under it, so it goes in the cart. Next to it is a large ceramic lamp tagged $8 with $40 comps, but shipping and packing would eat most of the gap, so you leave it.

Common sourcing mistakes

  • Pricing off asking prices instead of sold listings.
  • Buying on a hunch without checking comps.
  • Missing flaws that turn into returns.
  • Ignoring shipping on heavy or fragile items.
  • Buying too much slow stock that ties up cash for months.
  • Not recording cost, so you never know your real profit.
