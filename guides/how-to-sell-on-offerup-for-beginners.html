How to Sell on OfferUp for Beginners

OfferUp is a buy-and-sell app built around local deals, with an option to ship items to buyers across the country. It works well for bulky things that are a pain to ship, like furniture, tools, appliances and auto parts, and for everyday items you want gone quickly. This guide walks through your first sales step by step.

1. Set up and verify your profile

Use a real profile photo and name, confirm your phone and email, and complete any identity verification the app offers. Buyers on local apps lean heavily on trust, and a verified, complete profile gets more replies than a blank one.

2. Start with what you already own

List a few things from around the house first. It costs nothing, teaches you how messages, offers and meetups work, and earns your first ratings before you spend money on inventory. See how to start reselling with $100 for the next step.

3. Price from what actually sells nearby

Search OfferUp and Facebook Marketplace for the same item in your area, and check eBay sold listings for a national number. Local buyers expect to haggle, so leave a little room above the lowest price you will take. See how to price items to sell.

4. Take clear photos and write a searchable title

Shoot in good light from several angles, and show labels, model numbers and any flaws. Put the brand, model and what the item is in the title, the words a local buyer would type. Add size, condition and measurements in the description so people do not have to ask. See how to take listing photos and how to write listing titles.

5. Decide: local pickup, shipping, or both

Local pickup has no shipping cost and suits big or heavy items. Turning on shipping opens the item to buyers nationwide, but shipped sales carry a service fee and you need to pack the item well. Fees and rules change, so check OfferUp's current fee page before you price a shipped item. See how to pack items for shipping.

6. Answer messages fast and handle offers

Quick replies sell items, because local buyers often message several sellers at once. Expect lowball offers; counter politely with your real price instead of ignoring them. Keep the conversation in the app so you have a record. See how to handle offers.

7. Meet safely

Meet in a busy public place in daylight, such as a store parking lot or a police station's designated exchange spot if your area has one. Bring someone with you for big-ticket items, let the buyer inspect the item, and count cash or confirm in-app payment before you hand it over. Never accept overpayment or "send the extra back" requests.

8. Mark it sold and log the sale

Mark the item sold right away and end it anywhere else you cross-listed it. Write down the sale price, fees, how you were paid and any miles you drove to the meetup. Local sales are income just like online ones. See how to track side-hustle income and expenses and how to keep a mileage log for taxes.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

You list a used cordless drill kit for local pickup. Similar kits nearby are listed around $60 to $75, and sold ones on eBay went for about $55. You list it at $70, accept a $60 offer, and drive 6 miles to a store parking lot to meet. In your tracker you record the $60 sale, $0 fees for a local cash sale, and the 12 round-trip miles, not just the $60.

Common mistakes

  • Pricing with no room to haggle on an app where buyers expect to negotiate.
  • Slow replies, which send buyers to the next seller.
  • Meeting somewhere private instead of a busy public spot.
  • Forgetting shipping fees when you turn on nationwide shipping.
  • Not logging miles driven to meetups, which are a real cost.
