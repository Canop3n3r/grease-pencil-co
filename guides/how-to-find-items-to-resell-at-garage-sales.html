How to Find Items to Resell at Garage Sales and Estate Sales

Garage sales and estate sales are some of the cheapest places to buy inventory, because sellers want things gone, not top dollar. The catch is that you have to move fast, check prices on your phone, and know when to walk away. Here is a simple routine that works for weekend flippers.

1. Plan your route the night before

Check local listing sites, community groups and the classifieds for sales in your area. Look for words that hint at the kind of stuff you sell, such as "tools," "vintage," "moving," "estate" or "whole house." Map the sales so you are not zigzagging across town, and write down your mileage so you can count it as a sourcing cost. See how to keep a mileage log.

2. Bring the right kit

Carry small bills and coins, a charged phone, a tape measure, a small flashlight, batteries for testing electronics, and a bag or bin for small items. Having exact change makes haggling easier and keeps the line moving.

3. Know what to look for

Stick to categories you already understand at first. Common garage sale finds that resell well include hand and power tools, video games and consoles, name-brand clothing and shoes, kitchen gear, vintage décor, camera gear and car parts. Skip anything heavy and bulky unless you sell locally, because shipping can eat the profit. See how to ship items cheaply.

4. Check sold prices before you pay

Look up what the exact item has actually sold for, not what people are asking. If it has few recent sales compared with how many are listed, it may sit for months. See how to price items to sell and how to calculate sell-through rate.

5. Inspect it like you will have to describe it

Open boxes, count pieces, look for cracks, stains and missing parts, and test anything that plugs in or takes batteries when the seller allows it. Every flaw you find now is one you can price in and mention honestly in your listing, which helps avoid returns. See how to handle returns.

6. Set your max price, then haggle politely

Work out the most you can pay and still make the profit you want after fees, shipping and gas. If the sticker is above it, make a friendly offer; if it is still too high, thank them and move on. Asking for a price on a pile of items together often gets a better deal than haggling item by item. See how much to pay for items to resell and the free max buy price calculator.

7. Estate sales work a little differently

Estate sales are often run by companies with set prices that drop on later days. Early on day one gets you the best selection; the last day often brings big discounts on what is left. Read the sale's rules before you go, since some use sign-in sheets or numbers at the door.

8. Log every buy before you forget

On the drive home or as soon as you unload, write down each item, what you paid, where you bought it and the date. Split a bundle price across the items so each one has a cost. Then give each item a spot and a code so you can find it when it sells. See how to organize reseller inventory and how to track inventory in a spreadsheet.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

You hit four sales on a Saturday morning, driving 18 miles. At one you find a box of hand tools, a game console with two controllers and a pair of name-brand boots. The seller asks $40 for everything; you check sold prices, offer $30 for the lot, and they agree. You split the $30 by what each item should sell for, log all three with your miles, and list them that afternoon.

Common mistakes

  • Buying because it is cheap, not because it sells.
  • Skipping the inspection and finding the missing piece at home.
  • Forgetting gas and time when you add up the profit.
  • Not splitting bundle costs, so you cannot tell which item made money.
  • Letting the haul sit in the garage instead of listing it that week. See how to batch list items.
