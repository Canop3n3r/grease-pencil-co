When to Lower Your Price on eBay (What to Do With Stale Listings)

Every reseller ends up with a pile of listings that just sit. Stale inventory ties up your cash and your shelf space, and the longer it sits the more likely you are to forget what you paid. The fix isn't to slash everything. It's a simple routine: find out why it isn't selling, change one thing, and give it a set amount of time before the next step.

Platform tools like offers, price drops, promotions and relisting work differently on eBay, Poshmark and Mercari and change over time; check each platform's current seller help pages.

1. Know how long each listing has been up

You can't manage stale stock you can't see. Keep a list date for every item and a "days listed" column, so once a week you can sort by oldest first. See how to track reseller inventory in a spreadsheet. Pick your own checkpoints, for example 30, 60 and 90 days, and stick to them so you're not guessing every week.

2. Figure out which problem you have

Look at the listing's views or impressions and watchers, if the platform shows them, and compare against sold comps.

  • Few views: buyers aren't finding it. That's usually the title, item specifics or category, not the price. See how to write eBay listing titles that get found.
  • Views but no watchers or offers: buyers are seeing it and moving on. Check your main photo and your price against sold comps. See how to take eBay listing photos that sell.
  • Watchers but no sale: interest is there and the price or shipping cost is the sticking point. This is where a small drop or an offer to watchers usually helps.
  • Questions but no sale: the description is missing something. Add the answer to the listing. See how to write eBay item descriptions that sell.

3. Re-check the market before you drop

Pull fresh sold comps. Prices move, and the comps you used when you listed may be out of date. Also count how many similar items are listed right now versus how many sold recently; if there are far more listed than sold, expect to wait or to price near the low end. See how to price items to sell on eBay and how to calculate sell-through rate.

4. Drop in small steps, with a floor

Before you touch the price, write down your floor: the lowest price where you still make a profit you're happy with after fees, shipping and what you paid. Then lower in small steps and give each one time to work. Many sellers drop roughly 5 to 10 percent at each checkpoint rather than one big cut, because a big cut gives away money a smaller one might have recovered. Always check your floor with the free reseller profit calculator or eBay fee calculator.

5. Use offers when people are watching

If buyers are watching or liking the item, sending them a modest offer is often better than a public price drop, because it targets people who already want it. Accepting reasonable offers, with an auto-decline below your floor where the platform allows it, also moves items without you watching every message.

6. Refresh or relist

Sometimes a listing just needs new life: a better first photo, a clearer title, filled-in item specifics, or a fresh listing date. If you end and relist, update anything that's out of date, like the description or shipping weight.

7. Know when to cut your losses

If an item has sat through several drops and is near your floor, ask yourself if the cash and shelf space are worth more than the last few dollars. Bundling it with similar items, moving it to another platform, selling it locally, or donating it and keeping the receipt can all beat letting it sit for another year. What you paid is gone either way; decide based on what you can get now.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

You bought a jacket for $8 and listed it at $45 with free shipping. Your floor, after fees, shipping and what you paid, works out to $30. At 30 days it has a few views and no watchers, so you swap in a brighter main photo and add the brand's style name to the title. At 60 days it has two watchers, so you send them an offer at $40. At 90 days it's still there, so you lower the price to $38 and turn on offers with an auto-decline under $30. It sells for $36 on day 97, still above your floor.

Common stale-listing mistakes

  • Dropping the price when the real problem is that nobody can find the listing.
  • One big panic cut instead of small steps on a schedule.
  • No floor, so you end up selling at a loss without noticing.
  • Using old comps from when you first listed.
  • Forgetting shipping: a high shipping charge can stall a listing just like a high price.
  • Never checking, so items sit for a year before you look again.
