How to Sell on Mercari for Beginners

Mercari is a simple app for selling almost anything: clothes, electronics, toys, tools, home goods and collectibles. Listing takes a few minutes from your phone, and you can ship with a prepaid label. It is a good second marketplace if you already sell on eBay or Poshmark, and a fine first one if you are just starting.

1. Set up your account

Download the app, sign up, and add a profile photo and a short bio. Buyers trust sellers who look like real people. Add a bank account for payouts and turn on two-step sign-in to protect your account.

2. Check sold prices before you list

Search for your item and filter to sold items to see what it actually went for, not what people are asking. Price near the middle of recent sales for items in the same condition. See how to price items to sell; the same sold-comps method works on Mercari.

3. Take clear photos

Use natural light and a plain background. Show the front, back, labels, any model number and every flaw. The first photo is the one buyers see in search, so make it the cleanest shot. See how to take listing photos.

4. Write a title and description buyers search for

Put the brand, item type, model, size and color in the title, in the words a buyer would type. In the description, state the condition honestly, list every flaw and what is included, and add measurements for clothing. See how to write listing titles and how to write item descriptions.

5. Pick a shipping option

You can usually choose a prepaid Mercari label or ship on your own. Weigh the item packed, not bare, so you pick the right label size. Decide whether you or the buyer pays shipping and price the item to match. Ship quickly, since fast shipping helps your ratings. See how to pack items for shipping.

6. Know the fees before you set a price

Mercari takes a selling fee and there can be payment processing and payout costs. Fees change, so check Mercari's current fee page before pricing, and compare platforms in eBay vs Poshmark vs Mercari fees. Price so the amount left after fees and shipping still beats what you paid.

7. Handle offers and likes

Buyers often send offers below your price. Decide your lowest acceptable price before you list so you can answer quickly. When people like an item, you can lower the price a little to nudge them. See how to handle offers for a simple counter-offer approach.

8. Ratings matter

After each sale, the buyer rates you and you rate the buyer. Accurate descriptions, good packing and fast shipping are what earn five stars. A handful of good ratings early makes new buyers more willing to purchase.

9. Cross-list carefully

If the same item is listed on Mercari and other sites, remove it everywhere the moment it sells so you never sell one item twice. See how to cross-list on eBay, Poshmark and Mercari.

10. Track your real profit

Write down what you paid, the sale price, fees and shipping for every item. Your Mercari balance shows money in, not profit. See real profit after fees and how to track reseller inventory in a spreadsheet.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

You buy a pair of name-brand sneakers at a thrift store for $8. Sold listings show similar pairs going for about $40 to $50. You list at $48, accept a $42 offer two days later, and ship with a prepaid label. After you subtract the fees shown at checkout, the shipping cost and your $8, you write the actual profit in your tracker, not the $42 sale price.

Common mistakes

  • Pricing from active listings instead of sold ones.
  • Weighing the item without the box and picking a label that is too small.
  • Hiding flaws, which leads to returns and bad ratings.
  • Forgetting to delist a cross-listed item after it sells.
  • Counting the sale price as profit instead of what is left after fees and costs.
