How to Calculate Real eBay Profit After Fees (Simple Formula)

“Sold for $40, paid $8” sounds like $32 profit. It almost never is. Here’s the simple math for what you actually kept.

The formula

Real profit = Sale price − Platform fees − Shipping you paid − What you paid for the item − Supplies − Sourcing miles

1. Platform fees

On eBay, most categories charge a final value fee of roughly 13–15% of the total (item plus shipping), plus a small per-order fee. Poshmark takes a flat fee on sales under $15 and 20% on sales over $15. Mercari, Etsy and Facebook shipped orders have their own percentages. Fees change, so check each platform’s current fee page. Then use one number per platform, every time.

2. Shipping you paid

If you offered “free shipping” or the label cost more than the buyer paid, the difference comes out of your profit.

3. Cost of the item, plus supplies

Count the price tag plus anything you spent to make it sellable: cleaning supplies, a replacement part, poly mailers, tape.

4. Sourcing miles

A 20-mile thrift run isn’t free. Multiply your miles by a per-mile rate (many people use the IRS standard mileage rate) and split that cost across the items you bought on the trip.

Worked example

Sale price (incl. shipping charged)$48.00
eBay fees (≈13.6% + $0.40)−$6.93
Shipping label−$8.50
Item cost−$6.00
Mailer + cleaning−$1.00
Share of sourcing miles−$2.50
Real profit$23.07

That’s still a good flip, but it’s $23, not $40. Run that gap across a hundred sales and you’ll see why tracking matters.

Also track days held

$25 in 7 days and $25 in 200 days are not the same flip. Flag anything you’ve held 90+ days and drop the price, bundle it, or move on.
