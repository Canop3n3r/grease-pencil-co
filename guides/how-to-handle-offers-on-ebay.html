How to Handle Offers on eBay (When to Accept, Counter or Decline)

Offers are where a lot of reseller profit quietly disappears. A buyer sends a low number, you are tired of looking at the item, and you click accept without checking what you will actually take home. This guide gives you a simple rule: decide your floor before the offer arrives, then accept, counter or decline in seconds.

Offer features and fees differ by platform and change over time. Check each marketplace's current seller help pages and fees in its app before you rely on a setting.

1. Write down your floor when you list

Your floor is the lowest price you will take and still hit your minimum profit after fees, shipping and what you paid. Work it out once, when you list, and put it in your inventory sheet next to the list price. Then you never have to do math while a buyer waits. See how to calculate real eBay profit after fees and use the free reseller profit calculator.

2. Price with room for offers

Many buyers on eBay, Poshmark and Mercari expect to offer. If you list right at your floor, every offer is a no. A modest cushion above your floor, based on what similar items actually sold for, gives you room to say yes. Do not pad so much that your price stops matching sold comps, or buyers will scroll past. See how to price items using sold comps.

3. Accept fast when it clears your floor

If an offer is at or above your floor and close to recent sold prices, accept it. Holding out for a few more dollars on an item that clears your profit target usually costs more in time and stale stock than it earns. A sale today also frees up cash to buy your next inventory.

4. Counter instead of ignoring a low offer

A low offer still means someone wants the item. Counter somewhere between their number and your list price, and stay at or above your floor. Keep the message short and polite. If they counter back above your floor, take it. If they go below it, it is fine to stop there.

5. Decline what is below your floor

An offer below your floor loses you money or leaves you working for nothing. Decline it or counter at your floor, and do not take it personally. The exception is a truly stale item you have already decided to cut your losses on; see when to lower your price on eBay.

6. Use auto-accept and auto-decline where available

Some platforms let you set an amount to accept automatically and an amount to decline automatically. Set auto-decline at or just under your floor so lowball offers never reach you, and set auto-accept at a price you would happily take any time. Check the platform's current settings before you count on them.

7. Send offers to watchers and likers

When people are watching or liking an item but nobody buys, a small discount sent straight to them often closes the sale. Make sure the offer price, after fees and shipping, still clears your floor. This works best on items that are getting views but sitting a while.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

You paid $6 for a pair of boots. Sold comps cluster around $45, so you list at $49. Using example fee and shipping costs you looked up, you work out that $36 is the lowest price that still leaves your $15 minimum profit, so you write "floor $36" in the item's row. An offer comes in at $30: you counter at $42. The buyer counters at $38. That is above your floor, so you accept and mark the row sold with the final price.

Common offer mistakes

  • No floor written down, so you decide on a whim.
  • Forgetting fees and shipping when judging an offer.
  • Ignoring low offers instead of countering.
  • Listing right at your floor, leaving no room to negotiate.
  • Holding out for a few dollars on an item that already clears your profit.
