How to Sell on Whatnot for Beginners

Whatnot is a live shopping app where you sell in short auction-style shows instead of leaving a fixed listing up all day. Buyers watch, chat and bid in real time, so the energy of the show matters as much as the item. This guide walks through your first sales without guessing at fee rates that change.

Whatnot's seller rules, categories and fees change. Check Whatnot's current seller help and fee pages in the app before you go live.

1. Set up as a seller

Create your account, complete whatever identity and payout steps Whatnot asks for in your country, and fill in a clear shop name and bio that says what you sell, such as "vintage tees and sneakers, ships in 1 to 2 days." Confirm payouts before your first show so a sold item is not stuck waiting on paperwork.

2. Know what works in live shows

Categories with collectors and fans do well: sneakers, trading cards, vintage clothing, electronics, toys and branded accessories. Items that look good on camera and have clear sold comps elsewhere are easier to start with. Search sold prices on eBay or other apps first so you know a fair range before you set a start bid. See how to source items at thrift stores and how to inspect items before you list.

3. Prep your show before you go live

Group similar items into one show so buyers who care about that niche stay watching. Photograph tags, sizes and flaws ahead of time, write short titles, and set a start price and a personal floor for each piece. Lay the items in order next to your camera so you are not digging through a pile on air. Good light and a quiet room matter more than fancy gear.

4. Price from comps, then set a start bid

Look at recent sold prices for the same brand, model and condition, then work out what you need after Whatnot's seller fees, payment processing and shipping supplies. Fees change, so check Whatnot's current fee page. Start bids are often low to get people bidding; your floor is the lowest you will accept when the clock runs down. See how to price from sold comps and how platform fees compare.

5. Run a clear, honest show

Say the brand, size, condition and any flaws out loud before you start the auction. Show tags and problem spots on camera. Answer chat questions quickly. Keep a steady pace so quiet stretches do not empty the room, and do not hide flaws to chase a higher bid. Returns and bad ratings wipe out thin margins fast.

6. Ship fast after the show

Print labels through Whatnot's shipping flow or your own carrier as the app allows, pack each order the same day or next morning when you can, and update tracking. Fold clothing neatly, pad fragile items, and weigh packages so you are not surprised by postage. Fast shipping and clear packing earn the reviews that bring people back to your next show. See how to pack items for shipping and how to ship items cheaply.

7. End cross-listings and log every sale

If the same item was also up on eBay, Depop or another app, end those listings the moment it sells on Whatnot. Record what you paid, the hammer price, fees, shipping and supplies so you know real profit, not just the sale total. See how to cross-list without double-selling and how to calculate real profit after fees.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

You thrift a pair of sneakers for $18. Similar pairs sold for about $55 to $70 elsewhere. You start the auction at $20 with a personal floor of $40. It lands at $48. After Whatnot fees and a sturdy box, you log the real profit in your tracker, not just the $48 sale price.

Common mistakes

  • Going live with no floor, then accepting a bid that loses money after fees and shipping.
  • Skipping sold comps and guessing at start prices.
  • Hiding flaws on camera, which leads to returns and bad ratings.
  • Slow shipping after a busy show, which hurts your next audience.
  • Forgetting to end the same item on other platforms, which risks a double sale.
