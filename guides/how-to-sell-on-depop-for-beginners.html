How to Sell on Depop for Beginners

Depop is a resale app popular with younger buyers looking for vintage, streetwear, Y2K and one-of-a-kind clothing and accessories. Listings that look like a style feed do better than plain product shots, and buyers search by brand, era and aesthetic. This guide walks through your first sales step by step.

1. Set up your shop

Pick a clear shop name, add a profile photo and a short bio that says what you sell, such as "90s and Y2K vintage, sizes S to XL, ships in 1 to 2 days." Confirm your email and link a payout method before you list so your first sale is not held up.

2. Know what sells on Depop

Vintage tees, denim, sportswear, band and graphic shirts, statement accessories and recognizable streetwear brands tend to move. Plain fast-fashion basics usually sell better elsewhere or in bundles. Search Depop for an item and filter to sold listings to see what buyers actually pay. See how to source items at thrift stores.

3. Take styled, honest photos

Use daylight and a clean background, and include at least one photo of the item worn or laid out as an outfit, plus close-ups of tags, fabric and any flaws. The first photo is what shows in the feed, so make it the best one. See how to take listing photos.

4. Write a description buyers can search

Start with brand, item and era, then size, measurements (pit to pit and length for tops, waist and inseam for bottoms), condition and flaws. Add a few accurate style words like "vintage," "Y2K" or "streetwear" and fill in Depop's own category, brand, size and condition fields, since buyers filter by them. Keep it honest; style words that do not fit lead to returns. See how to write item descriptions.

5. Price from sold items, after fees

Look at sold Depop listings for the same brand and style, then work out what you keep after Depop's fees, payment processing and shipping. Fees change, so check Depop's current fee page before you set a price. Leave a little room above your floor because buyers often send offers. See how to price items using sold comps and how platform fees compare.

6. Handle offers, messages and bundles

Reply quickly and politely. Decide your lowest price before an offer comes in so you can accept or counter without guessing. Offer a bundle discount on multiple items from your shop; it moves more stock and saves on shipping. See how to handle offers and how to sell items in bundles.

7. Ship fast and pack it well

Use the shipping option Depop offers in your country or your own label, and set the parcel size honestly so you are not charged extra. Fold clothing neatly, use a poly mailer, and ship within the time you promised; fast shipping earns good reviews, and reviews sell the next item. See how to pack items for shipping.

8. Mark it sold and log the sale

End the item anywhere else you cross-listed it, then record what you paid, the sale price, fees, shipping and supplies. That is the only way to know your real profit per item. See how to cross-list without double-selling and how to calculate real profit after fees.

Example (made-up numbers for illustration only):

You find a 90s graphic tee at a thrift store for $4. Sold ones on Depop went for about $28 to $35. You list it at $34, accept a $30 offer, and the buyer pays shipping. After fees and a $0.50 mailer, you record your real profit in your tracker, not just the $30 sale price.

Common mistakes

  • Plain flat photos with no styled shot, which get scrolled past in the feed.
  • Missing measurements, since vintage sizing varies and buyers will ask or return.
  • Misleading style tags that bring in the wrong buyers.
  • Pricing from active listings instead of sold ones.
  • Forgetting fees and supplies when you work out profit.
